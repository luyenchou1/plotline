The nation's own test

NAEP, the National Assessment of Educational Progress, is the national test: the one assessment given the same way to a national sample of students since 1990, and the benchmark for how U.S. student performance is tracked over time and read against students in other countries. Scores run from 0 to 500; a ten-point move is about a year of learning. The tooltips also give the share of students at or above the Proficient standard.

Against the world

PISA tests 15-year-olds in about eighty countries every three years on the same scale. The OECD average is set near 500; thirty points is roughly a school year.

Reading this page

NAEP figures are the national public-school mean scale scores from the main assessments, pulled from the Nation's Report Card data service. 1990 to 1994 are the samples without testing accommodations; every later year includes them. Grade 8 reading was not assessed in 2000. The long-term-trend NAEP, which goes back to the early 1970s, is a different instrument and is not shown here.

PISA means are the OECD's published country averages, taken from Our World in Data for mathematics and reading and from the World Bank's mirror of the OECD tables for science, which stops at 2018; the 2022 science round will be added when a machine-readable copy is available. "OECD average" is the OECD's own figure for each round, so its membership changes over time. The U.S. 2018 reading result carries an OECD caveat about response rates. Rankings count OECD members with a score in that round.

Spending is current expenditure per pupil in fall enrollment from the NCES Digest of Education Statistics, table 236.55, in constant dollars of the latest school year in the table. Current expenditure leaves out construction and debt service. The international comparison is the OECD's expenditure per full-time-equivalent student in primary through post-secondary non-tertiary education, constant 2020 prices, converted at purchasing-power parity, from Education at a Glance via the OECD data API. Each source is re-fetched on a schedule; the stamp at the top is the last run.
