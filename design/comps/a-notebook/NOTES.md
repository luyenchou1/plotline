# Comp A: Field notebook

## Direction

Plotline as one curious person's working notebook about the world: faintly ruled paper, a true margin column for marginalia beside every section, entries listed by number and date, and a pinned note at the top of each plot. The charts are the rigorous part and sit on dot-grid graph paper, drawn in ink and pencil with one annotation color reserved for the aha and the milestones. Dark mode is the same notebook under a desk lamp: a warm dark paper with a faint lamp pool at the top-left, ink lightened to bone.

## Palette

| Role | Light | Dark |
|---|---|---|
| Paper (cool bone / lamp-lit paper) | `#ECEDE8` | `#2A2722` |
| Note card and chart paper | `#F5F6F2` | `#332F29` |
| Ink (text, the debt line) | `#1E211F` | `#E8E4DA` |
| Ink 2 (secondary text, ticks) | `#5B615E` | `#B3ADA0` |
| Pencil (the GDP line) | `#7F8785` | `#9A958A` |
| Ochre (the one annotation color: aha, milestones) | `#8A5A0A` | `#EBBD4A` |
| Rules and dot grid | ink at 6 to 18 percent | bone at 6 to 16 percent |

Ochre vs pencil was checked with the dataviz validator (normal-vision delta E 15.3 light, 19.2 dark; contrast above 3:1 on both papers). The ink and pencil pair is an emphasis scheme, so the validator's chroma-floor fail on the grey is by design.

## Type

Bricolage Grotesque (display: wordmark, headings, small uppercase labels) with Newsreader (body, and italic for marginalia and chart annotations) and Courier Prime (dates, figures, axis ticks, the ledger of hero numbers). The sans-display-over-serif-body order is the inverse of the usual pairing, which keeps the page from reading as an editorial template.

## What it does for the aha and the voice

The ochre is spent in exactly three places on the plot page: the "Feb 2013" figure in the ledger, the crossing marker with its handwritten-style callout, and the wash between the two lines after the crossing, so the eye lands on the crossover before it reads anything else. The voice lives in the apparatus rather than in decoration: "n.b." margin notes beside each section, a pinned note at the top of the entry, numbered entries with dates, and an index with dot leaders, all of which read as one person keeping notes rather than a publication.
